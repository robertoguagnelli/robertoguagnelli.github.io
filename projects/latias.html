04 / FLIGHT DYNAMICS · PYTHON + XFLR5

A dynamic instability changed the loaded CG target.

I assembled the longitudinal state matrix A in Python from stability derivatives and mass properties, and analyzed its eigenvalues to characterize the phugoid and short-period modes. I checked the model through comparison with XFLR5.

δẋ = A δx

Problem

Growing oscillations

The initial payload arrangement lowered the CG with little forward shift. Its modeled phugoid oscillations grew over time.

My decision

Move the loaded CG forward

I revised the loaded CG target and evaluated the longitudinal response again.

Result

Recover modeled stability

The revised loading arrangement recovered a stable response. This made dynamic behavior part of the payload-placement decision.

A conventional body-axis state ordering is shown to explain the method. This symbolic structure does not reproduce the original numerical matrix. The coefficients combine aerodynamic, inertial and trim effects; the last row expresses δθ̇ = δq.

Reading the eigenvalues

Decay and oscillation in one result.

det(λI − A) = 0

λ = σ ± iωd

A negative real part, σ, indicates a decaying mode. The imaginary part, ωd, gives its damped angular frequency. The two complex-conjugate pairs are interpreted as the phugoid and short-period modes.

For asymptotic stability, every eigenvalue of the longitudinal model must have a negative real part.

Modal quantities

ωn = √(σ² + ωd²)

ζ = −σ / ωn

Natural frequency, ωn
Characterizes the mode's underlying time scale.

Damping ratio, ζ
Describes decay relative to that natural frequency.

Damped frequency and natural frequency are distinct. No aircraft-specific eigenvalues, derivatives or modal values are published here.

The initial instability was identified from growing time-response oscillations. A proposed pendulum-like explanation was an engineering interpretation, not a verified causal mechanism.

06 / PAYLOAD SYSTEM

Retain each bottle. Cover the complete payload.

Bottle-count scoring → Rigid supports → Retainer iteration → Fabric cover → Installed system

01 / Iterate the PETG retainer

Problem: fractures occurred at the arm-to-base transition, where the bending moment was greatest.

My change: I revised the arm profile to become thinner farther from the base, while updating the outer radius and finger-access tabs.

Observed result: fractures stopped occurring in subsequent handling checks.

Repeated insertion cyclesProlonged static-load check
Retainer test scope

Repeated insertion and sustained-load checks informed the iteration. Insertion force was not measured, and long-term creep life was not established.

02 / Cover with fabric + Velcro

Rule: payload could not be directly exposed to airflow.

Response: cover the supported bottles with fabric secured by Velcro.

  • Negligible mass penalty
  • Rule compliance
  • Simple installation
  • Fits the existing support system

A lightweight, unconventional and rule-driven solution.

07 / PHYSICAL VALIDATION

Ground evidence, then flight.

My contribution: design the experiment

I proposed the wing-load test and designed the experiment to compare measured deflection with the structures team’s prediction. Wood anisotropy and variation between material batches made a physical check important.

Measured deflection exceeded the prediction

The test exposed a difference between the calculation and the built structure. Beam deflection and total wing deflection were interpreted separately; material variability was a consideration, not an isolated, proven cause.

Structural calculations: structures team. Assembly not tested to failure.
